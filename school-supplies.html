Community Initiative

Supplies for Students

Organizing a city-wide donation drive to make sure every child has the school supplies they need to learn and thrive.

Overview

What is Supplies for Students?

Supplies for Students is a community initiative I founded to address a gap I noticed in my own neighbourhood: many students were starting the school year without the basic tools they needed - notebooks, pencils, backpacks, and more. For families facing financial hardship, these costs add up quickly.

I organized a donation drive, working with local businesses, volunteers, and school administrators to collect and distribute hundreds of supplies to students across five schools. The goal was simple: no child should have to worry about having what they need to learn.

The Problem

Seeing the Need

It started with a conversation. A classmate mentioned that her younger siblings were sharing a single pencil between them at home because their family couldn't afford to buy more before the school year started. I assumed this was uncommon - but the more I asked around, the more I realized it wasn't.

Back-to-school supply costs can reach hundreds of dollars per child. For families stretched thin, that's an impossible ask. The result is students who show up on the first day already behind - not because of ability, but because of circumstance. I couldn't accept that that was just the way things are.

What I Did

Making It Happen

I started small - drafting a simple pitch and emailing five local businesses to ask if they'd be willing to act as donation drop-off points. Three said yes within a week. That early momentum gave me confidence to keep going.

I created a needs list in consultation with school counsellors, who helped me understand which supplies were most critical and which families were most in need. I then spread the word through social media, flyers, and direct outreach to community groups.

Over four weeks, I coordinated collection, sorting, and distribution with a team of eight volunteers. We organized supplies by grade level and partnered with school administrators to ensure items reached students confidentially and with dignity.

Impact

Reflection

What I Learned

This project taught me that community change doesn't require a big organization or a large budget - it requires someone willing to take the first step. What started as one email to five businesses turned into a drive that touched the lives of dozens of families.

I also learned how to work across different groups of people - from local shop owners to school administrators to teenage volunteers - each with different priorities and communication styles. Learning to listen, adapt, and bring people together around a shared goal was the most valuable skill I built.

Most importantly, this initiative reminded me why community matters. The warmth I felt handing a backpack to a child who lit up at receiving it - that's the kind of impact I want to keep creating, at whatever scale I can.
